Premium Career Playbook

CV එකේ content, ATS readability, achievements සහ final PDF quality වැඩිදියුණු කිරීමට පියවරෙන් පියවර මාර්ගෝපදේශය.

01 · Build a clear CV strategy

Start with the role, not the template. Read the job advert twice and identify the three outcomes the employer needs. Your CV should make those outcomes easy to find in the first 15 seconds. Keep the most relevant evidence near the top, remove unrelated detail, and use a consistent story across the headline, summary, skills and experience. A visually attractive CV still needs a clear professional direction.

Before editing, write one sentence describing the role you are targeting. Then select only the evidence that supports that direction. Create a separate version when applying for a genuinely different career path rather than forcing one generic CV to cover every role.

02 · Write a strong professional summary

A useful summary is specific, brief and evidence-led. Aim for three or four sentences: your professional identity, your strongest relevant capabilities, one or two outcomes, and the value you can bring to the target role. Avoid empty claims such as “hard-working”, “dynamic” or “team player” unless you immediately prove them with an example.

Formula: “[Role / level] with [relevant experience or focus]. Skilled in [two or three job-related strengths]. Delivered [measurable outcome or scope]. Looking to contribute [relevant value] in [target role / environment].” Do not claim years of experience, qualifications or results you cannot verify.

03 · Turn duties into achievements

A duty explains what was assigned; an achievement explains what changed because of your work. Start bullets with a precise action verb, add the project or challenge, and finish with the result. Include scale, speed, quality, revenue, cost, customer impact or risk reduction where evidence exists. Use numbers only when you can explain how they were measured.

Weak: “Responsible for social media.” Stronger: “Planned a four-channel content calendar and improved qualified enquiries by 22% over one quarter.” Weak: “Worked on a website.” Stronger: “Rebuilt the mobile checkout flow and reduced abandoned checkout sessions by 14%.” Adapt every example to your real work.

04 · Use the STAR / CAR method

STAR means Situation, Task, Action and Result. CAR means Challenge, Action and Result. These frameworks help you remember the context, but the final CV bullet should usually be concise. Keep the action and result prominent; the reader does not need a long story. Your Achievement Bullet Coach can help create a first draft, which you should then fact-check and refine.

Prompt yourself: What was difficult? What were you personally responsible for? What did you change or build? Which tools or people were involved? What improved? How do you know? If no numeric metric exists, describe a concrete qualitative result such as fewer hand-offs, faster approval, clearer reporting or improved accessibility.

05 · Skills and keyword alignment

Use the same terminology as the job advert when it truthfully describes your experience. Include relevant tools, methods, certifications and domain knowledge in context. A keyword list alone is weak evidence: show where you used the skill in a project or role. Group skills into sensible categories and remove skills that are outdated or too basic for the role.

The Job Match Scanner identifies possible overlap and missing terms; it is a drafting aid, not an official ATS score. Read every suggested keyword. Add only terms that match your actual capability, and use the correct level (familiar, working knowledge, advanced, certified). Never hide keywords in tiny text or add irrelevant keyword blocks.

06 · Education, projects and early-career CVs

If you are a student or recent graduate, education, practical projects, internships, volunteering and relevant coursework can carry more weight than employment history. Describe the purpose of each project, your individual contribution, tools used and the outcome. Link to a portfolio or repository only when it is public, relevant and presentable.

For experienced candidates, move education below recent relevant work unless the qualification is essential or unusually relevant. Include institution, qualification and completion year. Include grades only when they strengthen the application or are requested. Do not invent project clients, team sizes, awards or responsibilities.

07 · ATS-friendly structure

Applicant Tracking Systems vary. A simple, readable structure generally reduces parsing problems: recognizable section headings, selectable text, consistent dates, conventional contact details and a sensible reading order. Avoid relying on icons alone to communicate phone, email or links. Check the exported PDF by selecting and copying text, and keep a clean version available if an employer requests DOCX.

Some employers use multi-column or designed CVs successfully, but complex tables, text embedded in images, unusual symbols and decorative layouts can be harder for some systems to parse. When applying through an online portal, follow its file-type and formatting instructions. Do not assume every ATS rejects columns or every PDF is safe.

08 · Length, readability and proofing

There is no universal page-count rule. Use the space needed to show relevant evidence without repetition. Early-career candidates often need less space than senior specialists with substantial relevant achievements. Prioritize recent and relevant roles, keep bullet lengths balanced, use readable type and leave enough white space. A dense CV is not automatically a stronger CV.

Proofread names, dates, email addresses, phone numbers, links, punctuation and tense. Use present tense for current responsibilities and past tense for completed work. Ask another person to review the exported PDF on a phone and a desktop. Confirm that page breaks do not split headings from their content and that no text is clipped.

09 · Choosing a template and color theme

Choose a template based on the role and the reader. Conservative layouts are often suitable for finance, operations, administration and formal corporate roles. Creative layouts can help in design, media and portfolio-led work, provided the content remains readable. A theme is a visual layer; it cannot compensate for weak evidence or inconsistent information.

Before exporting, inspect every page at normal zoom. Check contrast, heading hierarchy, alignment, spacing and whether the most important evidence appears early. If a template looks impressive but makes dates, employers or skills difficult to scan, choose a simpler one. Keep color use restrained and make sure the document remains readable when printed in grayscale.

10 · Contact details, privacy and references

Use a professional email address and a phone number that you can answer. Check that portfolio and social links work and point to the correct profile. Include a city or region when it helps the employer understand location; a full residential address is usually unnecessary unless specifically requested. Remove personal details that are not relevant to the application.

Do not publish identity numbers, bank details, passwords, private references’ contact information or sensitive personal data in a public portfolio. Ask referees for permission before sharing their details. If you prefer, state that references are available on request. Review photos and public links before sharing your CV widely.

11 · Tailor applications and maintain versions

Keep a reliable master CV with your complete history, then create a targeted copy for each role family. Tailor the summary, order of skills and first achievements to the job requirements. Do not rewrite your entire history for every application; focus on relevance and evidence. Use the Version Snapshots feature to save meaningful variants before making major changes.

Name versions clearly, for example “Frontend Engineer — Product Teams” or “Operations Manager — Logistics”. Record the date and role. Before sending, verify that the filename is professional, the PDF opens correctly and the contact details match the application form. Remove comments, tracked changes and accidental draft notes.

12 · Final submission checklist

Confirm the job title and company name, check that every claim is accurate, test all links, proofread spelling and grammar, and review the exported file on a small screen. Ensure the document contains no leftover example text, template instructions or placeholder content. If the employer requests a specific format or naming convention, follow it exactly.

A good CV is a truthful, focused introduction—not a guarantee of an interview. Keep a record of the role, application date, submitted version and follow-up date. Prepare short examples for the achievements on your CV so you can explain your contribution clearly during an interview.

13 · Portfolio and online presence

Your portfolio should make it easy to understand what you can do and what you personally contributed. Select a small number of strong examples rather than linking to every experiment you have made. For each project, explain the problem, your role, the decisions you made, the tools you used and the outcome. Include screenshots or demonstrations only when you have permission to share them.

Check every public link in a private browser window before applying. Remove broken pages, unfinished placeholder projects and confidential client material. If a repository is public, add a clear README, setup instructions and a short explanation of the architecture. If you cannot share the work publicly, describe the project at a high level without revealing private information.

14 · Write a tailored cover letter

A cover letter should add context, not repeat the CV line by line. Explain why this particular role interests you, connect two relevant strengths to the employer’s needs, and give one concise example of impact. Keep the tone professional and direct. Address the hiring manager by name only when you have verified the correct name and spelling.

Use the Cover Letter Studio to create an editable starting draft. Replace generic sentences with specific reasons, check the company name and job title, and remove any claim that is not supported by your experience. If the application does not request a cover letter, follow the employer’s instructions rather than attaching unnecessary documents.

15 · Prepare for interview questions

For each major achievement on your CV, prepare a short explanation of the situation, your personal responsibility, the action you took and the result. Be ready to explain the numbers, tools, trade-offs and lessons learned. Distinguish your own contribution from the work of the wider team. Honest detail is more convincing than an inflated claim.

Prepare examples of problem-solving, collaboration, communication, learning from a mistake and handling competing priorities. Review the employer’s products and the role requirements. Prepare thoughtful questions about the team’s goals, success measures, onboarding and working practices. Do not memorize a script word for word; practice clear, natural answers.

16 · Explain a career change or employment gap

For a career change, lead with transferable skills and evidence that connects your previous work to the new role. Show relevant projects, training, volunteering or practical work. Do not disguise your past job titles; explain the connection clearly and focus on the value you can bring to the new team.

Employment gaps do not need lengthy explanations on the CV. Use accurate dates and include relevant study, caregiving, freelance work, travel, personal projects or other activities only when you are comfortable sharing them. Never invent employment or alter dates to conceal a gap. Prepare a brief, factual explanation for an interview if asked.

17 · International and remote applications

Application conventions differ by country and employer. Follow the vacancy instructions about file type, language, date format, work authorization and location. Do not assume that a photo, date of birth, marital status or full home address is required. Include work authorization or relocation information only when relevant and accurate.

For remote roles, make collaboration evidence visible: written communication, documentation, asynchronous work, time-zone coordination, ownership and delivery. Mention tools only when you have used them. Keep your contact details internationally readable and include a country code with your phone number when useful.

18 · Keep your CV accurate over time

Update your master CV when you complete a project, receive a qualification, change responsibilities or deliver a measurable result. Record the date and evidence while the details are fresh. Keep a private record of the source for important metrics so you can explain them later. Remove outdated skills and revise your headline when your target direction changes.

Before sharing a new version, check that it does not contain confidential information, private client data or contact details belonging to someone else. Save a named snapshot, export the PDF and open the final file to confirm that it is the exact version you intended to send.

19 · Technical and specialist CVs

Technical candidates should make their contribution understandable to both specialist and non-specialist readers. Name the system, product or workflow, describe the scale or constraint, identify your role and explain the outcome. Mention technologies in the context of work rather than presenting a long unprioritized list. For engineering roles, include architecture, reliability, testing, security, performance, maintainability and collaboration evidence where relevant.

For design roles, explain the problem, research, constraints, design decisions, iteration and impact. For finance or operations, show accuracy, controls, process improvement, reporting, risk management and stakeholder outcomes. For sales or marketing, explain audience, channel, pipeline, conversion, retention and return on investment where the data is available. Adapt the evidence to the actual role rather than copying a generic list.

20 · Senior, leadership and management CVs

Senior candidates should show scope, decisions and outcomes—not only a list of responsibilities. Explain the size and purpose of the function you led, the stakeholders involved, the complexity of the work and the improvements achieved. Include strategy, delivery, budget, hiring, mentoring, governance and change leadership only where they reflect your actual responsibilities.

Make individual accountability clear while giving appropriate credit to teams. A strong leadership bullet might describe the challenge, the decision or operating change you led, and the resulting improvement in delivery, quality, customer outcomes or capability. Avoid vague claims such as “visionary leader” without supporting evidence. Keep older roles concise when recent work is more relevant.

21 · Responsible use of metrics and claims

Metrics can strengthen a CV, but inaccurate precision can damage trust. Use a figure only when you know its source, timeframe and your relationship to the result. Distinguish “led”, “contributed to”, “supported” and “delivered”. If the result came from a team, say so. If exact data is confidential, use an approved range or describe the improvement qualitatively without exposing protected information.

Do not claim certifications before they are awarded, list tools you have never used, exaggerate language proficiency or present a group achievement as solely your own. Keep a private evidence note for important outcomes so you can discuss them confidently. Accuracy is more valuable than a dramatic but unsubstantiated statement.

22 · PDF export and final technical checks

After exporting, open the PDF in a separate viewer rather than relying only on the live preview. Inspect every page at fit-to-width and normal reading size. Confirm that text is selectable, links work, fonts render correctly and no content is clipped at the page edges. Check that the file is not password-protected unless requested and that the file size is reasonable for the employer’s portal.

Use a clear filename such as “FirstName_LastName_TargetRole_CV.pdf”. Avoid names like “final-final-newest.pdf”. Keep the JSON backup as an editable working copy and the PDF as the version you submit. If the export looks different from the preview, simplify the layout, review page spacing and export again before sending.
